RT-583 · built-in themes and the new default

Our main window drawn in eleven themes: today's Contrast for reference, then herdr-gpui's built-ins and bundled Ghostty schemes, each turned into our UI tokens by herdr-gpui's own chrome derivation. Pick the set to ship and the default. Every hex below is computed, not hand-tuned.

How a scheme becomes our tokens

herdr-gpui derives its chrome from three things in a scheme: background, foreground and the 16-colour palette. Its blend is mix(base, over, pct), an integer per-channel blend of pct% of over onto base (crates/herdr-gpui/src/config/theme.rs:366-373). Its ink(c) moves a colour's OKLab lightness, keeping its hue, only as far as it needs to reach 3:1 against the background, surface and active fills (theme.rs:509-515, contrast.rs:154-181; 3:1 is the Standard contrast level, contrast.rs:32-37). Paths are under X:\dev\_ext\penso\herdr-gpui.

herdr's Default is hand-authored and skips the derivation: its surface #1c1c22, active #2b2933 and muted #827e91 are fixed (theme.rs:348-362), its palette is the xterm VGA set, and its accent is that palette's dark magenta #800080, inked. The rest of that lane follows the rules above.

Claude's own lines in the top pane (✻ Brewing…, ⏵⏵ accept edits on) are truecolor Claude Code prints whatever the scheme, so they do not change between lanes. The flags under each lane hold our floors from palette.rs's contrast test (TEXT 7:1 on GROUND, TEXT_2 and SUBTLE 4.5:1 on every surface they sit on), ANSI colours under 3:1 on the terminal ground, colours whose OKLab distance is under 0.12 (easily confused), and two status colours whose OKLab hues sit under 35° apart (they read as one colour at glyph size).
